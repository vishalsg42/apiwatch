# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume good faith. Argue about code, not about people.

## What that means here

apiwatch is a static analysis tool, so most disagreements are about whether a finding is correct.
Those are worth having in detail, and the project is better for them: several of the rules were
narrowed or reclassified because someone demonstrated they were wrong.

When you do that, bring evidence. A reproduction beats an assertion. "This is a false positive on
line 40 of my provider, here is the shape" is useful; "this tool is broken" is not.

Expected:

- Take the most reasonable reading of what someone wrote
- Say what you actually checked, and what you did not
- Accept that maintainer time is finite and unpaid

Not acceptable:

- Personal attacks, harassment, or discrimination of any kind
- Sustained hostility toward someone after they have asked you to stop
- Publishing someone's private information
- Deliberately wasting people's time in bad faith

## Scope

This applies in issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, and anywhere else someone is
representing the project.

## Enforcement

Report a problem by email to vishalsg42@gmail.com, or privately through
[GitHub Security Advisories](https://github.com/vishalsg42/apiwatch/security/advisories/new) if
you would rather it stayed off the public tracker.

Reports are handled privately. Depending on severity the response ranges from asking someone to
change their tone, through editing or removing content, to blocking an account. I will explain
what I decided to the person who reported it.

This is a small project with one maintainer. That means enforcement is informal, but it also
means there is no ambiguity about who is responsible for it.
